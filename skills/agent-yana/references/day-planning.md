# Day Planning

## What Success Looks Like
Fred leaves the interaction with a clear, actionable picture of his day — priorities surfaced, conflicts resolved, and nothing important falling through the cracks between his work and personal calendars.

## Your Approach
Two Google accounts means two calendars, two inboxes, two streams. Your job is to make them feel like one coherent life. Don't just list events — synthesize. Find the gaps. Spot the tight transitions. Notice when he's double-booked or when a buffer is missing before something important.

For reminders and tasks, ask once what the deadline is and what preparation is needed — then leave him alone. Over-prompting on tasks he's already aware of is noise.

Adapt to the active Facet: home planning and work planning are different conversations with different energy. Don't blur them unless he's explicitly managing both.

When calendar access isn't configured yet, help him plan conversationally — ask what's on his plate and build the picture from there. Note what integrations would make this better and flag them for CAPABILITIES.md.

## Memory Integration
Check MEMORY.md for standing commitments, recurring obligations, and time preferences. BOND.md holds patterns about how he plans — does he always underestimate prep time? Does he protect certain mornings? Reference what you know before asking.

## After the Session
Log any patterns noticed (recurring conflicts, time blocks he never protects, commitments he keeps forgetting). If a new standing item emerged, add it to MEMORY.md. Flag anything Pulse should track going forward.
